## Data format

`F(title, year, director, "Genre/Genre", theaterKey, "ind"|"maj", showtimes, opts)`

- `showtimes`: `"day@HHMM,day@HHMM"` using DAY-OF-MONTH numbers and 24-hour times, e.g. `"7@1930,8@1415"`. Never use weekday abbreviations like `fri@1930` — the app cannot parse them and the screening disappears.
- `showtimes` is `null` only when no times are published; then set `opts.r=[startDay,endDay]` (endDay > 31 means the run continues into next month) plus `opts.note` such as "Dates vary — see site".
- `opts` also takes `fmt` ("35mm"/"70mm"/"4K restoration"/"IMAX 70mm"/"16mm"), `ser` (series name), `note`, `tix` (a direct ticket/booking URL for this specific film — see below).
- `"ind"`/`"maj"` describes the FILM's distribution, not the venue.
- `tix`: when you're on a theater's site pulling a film's showtimes, you're usually already on (or one click from) that film's own ticket page — capture that URL as `opts.tix` instead of leaving the listing to fall back to the theater's generic homepage. This is the single biggest lever for cutting the time between someone reading a listing and buying a ticket, so treat it as part of the core research, not an afterthought. Priorities, in order: (1) a URL that lands on the exact showtime already selected, if the booking system supports that in the link (many chain and Fandango-style systems do — look for a `date=`/`time=`/session-id parameter); (2) a URL for that specific film's own page, from which the real showtime picker is one click away; (3) omit `tix` entirely and let it fall back to `THEATERS[key].u` — never guess or construct a URL you haven't actually loaded and confirmed shows that film. A wrong ticket link is worse than the generic homepage fallback.
- Chain `tix` links (AMC, Regal, Alamo) are usually tied to a specific showtime ID for a specific day, not a stable per-film page — unlike indie theaters (Film Forum, Metrograph, IFC, etc.), which typically have a durable per-film URL that doesn't expire. That means chain `tix` links go stale after their date passes and need refreshing on every run, not just once. When you touch a chain record for its showtime data, re-verify/re-fetch its `tix` link at the same time rather than assuming yesterday's is still good — and if the film isn't currently scheduled at that specific theater location (chains often show a title at only some of their locations, not all), leave `tix` off entirely rather than pointing at a different location's showing.
- `year` is the film's real original production/premiere year — verify it, never default to the current year, the year you're writing the record in, or the year of THIS PARTICULAR theatrical run. Distributors frequently give a film a delayed U.S./NYC release a year or more after its actual festival premiere (e.g., a film that premiered at TIFF/Venice/SXSW in one year but doesn't open in NYC theaters until the next) — use the film's real original premiere year (what IMDb/Wikipedia list in the infobox), not the year it happens to be opening at the theater you're looking at. A "restoration," "revival," "anniversary," or "retrospective" screening is by definition an OLD film; if you can't quickly confirm its actual year, that's a sign to go look it up, not to leave today's year in place.
- This applies at ANY venue, not just outdoor/park series: any listing that's actually a multi-work program — a retrospective spanning a director's whole career, a themed series pairing films from different decades, a shorts compilation, an audience-choice night between unrelated films — has no single correct release year by definition. Use `NaN` (bare, unquoted) rather than any year for these, whether it's an outdoor park series (Movies Under the Stars, SummerScreen, Flicks on the Beach, RiverFlicks, Rooftop Films Summer Series, Socrates Outdoor Cinema) or an indoor venue's own series (a BAM/Anthology/Quad retrospective, a MoMI Dolby Atmos series, etc.) — the test is "does this listing represent one specific film," not which venue it's at. The app already renders `NaN` as "—" and correctly excludes it from age-based sorting and the pre-1990 count; do not substitute `0`, `null`, or an arbitrary/guessed year for these.
- If you cannot find ANY corroborating information for a title anywhere (not the year — the film itself, its existence, any synopsis or cast) after a real search, that's a sign the record may be wrong or fabricated, not just under-researched. Flag it rather than silently keeping or guessing a year for it.

Never invent a showtime. If a theater publishes a weekly grid without a per-day breakdown, apply it across the run days and say so in the note. Drop records whose dates have all passed; add newly announced ones; keep existing good records unless you have fresher information for that same screening. Independent-theater revivals of classic and older films are the heart of this product; chains are supporting cast.
